# Summary

A platform helps only when teams use it, so a platform team measures adoption, the first experience, the work that the platform removes and the opinion of its users. Each measure needs a clear population, a stated definition and the count behind the number.

Adoption is the number of eligible teams that use a capability divided by the number of eligible teams. Teams that the platform cannot serve are not eligible and stay out of the denominator. The report states whether adopted means ever onboarded or active in a period, and it separates mandated teams from teams that chose the platform, because a mandate produces adoption without showing value.

Onboarding runs from a defined first request to a defined first successful deployment. It is reported with the median, a high percentile and the number of teams that did not finish, because the teams that are still stuck are the ones with the worst experience. Small groups give weak evidence for a comparison.

The self-service rate divides self-service requests by all requests, including manual tickets. Tickets show the steps that stay manual, and an estimate of their cost, built from stated handling times, helps a team decide what to automate first.

Satisfaction comes from a survey. The customer satisfaction score is the share of respondents who are satisfied, and the Net Promoter Score is the share of promoters minus the share of detractors. Both are reported with the number of respondents. A platform run as a product closes the feedback loop by telling users what changed.

Key facts:

- Adoption rate = adopted eligible teams / eligible teams x 100.
- NPS = (promoters minus detractors) / respondents x 100, with promoters at 9 to 10 and detractors at 0 to 6.
- Self-service rate = self-service requests / (self-service requests + manual tickets) x 100.
- Mandated adoption does not show that a platform is useful.
